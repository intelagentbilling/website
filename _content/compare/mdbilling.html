In short

MDBilling.ca no longer exists as a separate product. RBC acquired it on 4 October 2022 and merged it into Dr.Bill, which RBC had acquired in October 2019. As of October 2026, mdbilling.ca/pricing redirects to dr-bill.ca. If you are shopping MDBilling against anything, you are shopping Dr.Bill.

This page exists because a lot of Ontario physicians still search for MDBilling.ca, and the comparison they are expecting is no longer the comparison that exists.

What happened

  • October 2019 — RBC acquires Dr.Bill.
  • 4 October 2022 — RBC acquires MDBilling.ca. The announcement describes more than 30 MDBilling staff joining, and Dr.Bill claiming 5,000+ clients at the time.
  • Since — MDBilling has been merged into Dr.Bill. The mdbilling.ca pricing URL redirects to dr-bill.ca.

The practical consequence for a physician choosing software today is that what was once a three-way choice between Dr.Bill, MDBilling and everyone else is now a two-way choice between RBC's product and the independents.

If you are an existing MDBilling customer

Nothing on this page is advice about your current arrangement — Dr.Bill operate the service and are the people to ask about it.

What is worth knowing is how little is locked in. Your OHIP billing number, your GO Secure account, your EDT enrolment and the bank account the ministry deposits into all belong to you and stay exactly where they are. Changing providers means changing which designee is authorised to submit on your behalf — one screen in your own MCEDT profile.

The sequence that avoids losing claims in the handover is set out in switching OHIP billing software. The short version: add the new designee before removing the old one, and let one full cycle settle before you cut anything off.

Export first. Whatever you decide, get your patient list and claim history out while your account is still active. Access usually ends when billing does, and a patient roster with current version codes is worth several hours of re-validation.

The independent options

Ontario still has independent providers: IntelAGENT, SnapBill, MedConcierge and CAB.md. They differ on mobile, on caps, on group billing and on whether they publish pricing at all. All of them are laid out in OHIP billing software in Ontario, compared and Dr.Bill alternatives.

Frequently asked questions

Is MDBilling.ca still in business?

Not as an independent product. RBC acquired MDBilling.ca on 4 October 2022 and merged it into Dr.Bill, which RBC had acquired in October 2019. As of October 2026 the mdbilling.ca pricing page redirects to dr-bill.ca.

Who bought MDBilling.ca?

RBC, announced on 4 October 2022. RBC had already acquired Dr.Bill in October 2019, and MDBilling was merged into Dr.Bill with more than 30 MDBilling staff joining the combined business.

Do I need to do anything if I was an MDBilling customer?

That is a question for Dr.Bill, who now operate the service. If you are considering moving elsewhere, the mechanics are straightforward: your OHIP billing number, GO Secure account and EDT enrolment stay with the ministry, and switching means changing which designee is authorised to submit for you.

What is the IntelAGENT equivalent of MDBilling?

IntelAGENT is an independent Ontario OHIP billing platform operating since 2016, used by more than 2,000 physicians. Self-service is 0.25% of paid claims capped at $39.99 a month, and Premium is 2% with a dedicated billing agent. The first three months are free.

In short

Dr.Bill and IntelAGENT both charge 0.25% of paid claims on their entry tier. IntelAGENT caps that at $39.99 a month; Dr.Bill publishes no cap. Dr.Bill is owned by RBC and operates in more than one province; IntelAGENT is independent, has operated since 2016, is Ontario-only, and is built around hospital, group and departmental billing. Both have native mobile apps.

Everything on this page comes from each company's own published material, checked on 11 October 2026. We have not rated Dr.Bill, counted their reviews, or described features we cannot source to them.

The cap, in numbers

Both entry tiers are 0.25%. Below roughly $16,000 of paid claims a month they cost the same thing. Above it, one stops and one does not.

Whether this matters depends entirely on your volume. For a part-time practice it is immaterial. For a full-time hospital-based physician it is the difference between a fixed cost and a percentage of everything you earn, forever.

The full-service tiers

Dr.Bill's Comprehensive is 1.95%; IntelAGENT's Premium is 2%. Within five hundredths of a percentage point, these are the same price, and the choice between them is not an arithmetic one.

The structural difference is at the entry tier. Dr.Bill publishes an hourly rate for billing-agent help on Essentials — $50/hour at the time of writing — which means rejection handling on the cheap tier has a meter running. IntelAGENT's Self-Service tier includes support without an hourly charge.

If your rejection volume is low, this is theoretical. If you work in a setting that generates rejections — out-of-province patients, frequent card replacements, referral-heavy consulting — it is worth asking Dr.Bill how often Essentials users end up on the hourly rate.

Ownership and the MDBilling merge

RBC acquired Dr.Bill in October 2019 and MDBilling.ca in October 2022, merging the latter into the former. If you are comparing Dr.Bill with MDBilling.ca, see MDBilling.ca vs IntelAGENT — they are now the same product.

Bank ownership brings resources and brings a strategic owner whose priorities are not only billing software. IntelAGENT is independent and has been doing one thing since 2016. Neither of those is a verdict; they are different situations and physicians weigh them differently.

Hospital groups and departments

This is where the two products are genuinely built differently rather than priced differently.

IntelAGENT is built around hospital work: billing under a ministry group number, splitting the group's remittance back to the physician who rendered each service including reversals, and departmental reporting across a roster of physicians. See departments and groups.

Dr.Bill publishes support for 60+ specialties and operates in multiple provinces, which is a different kind of breadth. If your practice spans provinces, that is a point in its favour that IntelAGENT cannot match.

The question to put to both. "When OHIP reverses a group claim with a 55 and re-pays it with a 57 in a later cycle, which physician's month does each leg land in?" The answer tells you whether group handling is real or nominal.

Which to choose

  • You practise in more than one province — Dr.Bill, on geography alone.
  • You bill at volume and want a fixed cost — the cap is the deciding factor.
  • Most of your billing is hospital group or departmental work — ask both, but this is what IntelAGENT is built for.
  • You bill from a phone at the bedside — both have apps; try both, since this is the thing you will touch every day.
  • You want a billing agent — the two full-service tiers are within a rounding error of each other on price, so decide on fit.

Frequently asked questions

Is Dr.Bill owned by RBC?

Yes. RBC acquired Dr.Bill in October 2019, and in October 2022 acquired MDBilling.ca and merged it into Dr.Bill. IntelAGENT is independently owned and has been operating since 2016.

Does Dr.Bill cap its monthly fee?

Dr.Bill does not publish a monthly cap on its Essentials tier as of October 2026 — the rate is stated as 0.25% of paid claims. IntelAGENT's self-service tier is also 0.25% but caps the monthly charge at $39.99, so the fee stops rising once paid claims pass roughly $16,000 a month.

What is the difference between Dr.Bill Essentials and Comprehensive?

Essentials is the self-service tier at 0.25% of paid claims, with billing-agent help available at an hourly rate. Comprehensive is 1.95% of paid claims and includes a billing team. IntelAGENT's equivalent tiers are Self-Service at 0.25% capped at $39.99 and Premium at 2% with a dedicated billing agent.

Which is better for hospital and group billing?

IntelAGENT is built around group and departmental billing, including splitting a group remittance back to the physician who rendered each service. Dr.Bill publishes support for 60+ specialties and operates in more than one province. If your billing is mostly hospital group work, ask both providers specifically how reversals are attributed within a group.

Emergency billing follows a consistent pattern: the clinical decisions are not the hard part, the time arithmetic is. A single overnight shift can span three time bands, and the code that applies changes at each boundary.

The time bands are the whole game

Unlike most of the Schedule, emergency assessment codes do not work as a base code plus a premium. The time band is baked into the code. H103A and H153A describe the same assessment; the difference is that one happened on a Tuesday afternoon and the other on a Saturday.

That design has two consequences:

  • A shift crossing a boundary uses more than one code. An overnight shift starting Friday evening produces claims under the Friday-evening code and the nights code, split at the boundary, not one code for the whole shift.
  • Getting the band wrong is invisible on the Remittance Advice. Billing the weekday code for a Saturday patient pays — just less. Nothing is rejected, so nothing tells you.

This is the single largest avoidable loss in emergency billing, and it is the clearest case for not choosing the code at all. Critical care units

G395A covers the first 15 minutes of critical care; G391A covers each subsequent 15-minute unit. The time must be documented and must have been devoted to that patient.

Two failure modes, in opposite directions:

  • Under-claiming. A 70-minute resuscitation documented as such supports G395A plus three units of G391A. Claiming G395A alone because the units were not counted is common and costs more than any other single omission in the specialty.
  • Over-claiming. Units beyond what the record supports get rejected or recovered on audit. The documented time is the ceiling.

Before submitting critical care: the documented start and end time should divide into the number of units claimed, and the note should make clear the time was devoted to that patient and not shared across the department.

Special visit premiums

Special visit premiums are a separate mechanism from the time-band assessment codes, and they come up when you are called in rather than when you are on a scheduled shift. The rules on which premium applies, how many can be claimed for one visit, and which companion codes are required are set out in the Schedule and are specific enough that guessing produces rejections.

We have written the structure up separately rather than publishing a premium-code table, because the eligibility conditions matter more than the codes: how special visit premiums work.

What gets missed

  1. Time-band codes on shifts that cross midnight

    Patients seen at 23:50 and 00:10 are different codes. Manual entry almost always bills both the same way.

  2. Critical care units against documented time

    Count the units from the chart, not from memory at the end of the shift.

  3. The ECG professional component

    $4.55 is small. Across a year of shifts it is not, and it is a pure omission — you did the interpretation either way.

  4. Interim admission orders

    H105A exists for the work you do writing orders on a patient who is admitted but still in your department.

Frequently asked questions

Which OHIP codes do emergency physicians bill most?

The multiple systems assessment family (H103A, H153A and the evening and night variants), critical care units G395A and G391A, and the ECG professional component G313A. Which assessment code applies is determined by the time and day of the encounter, not by the complexity.

How do emergency department premiums work?

The assessment code itself changes with the time band — weekday day, Monday to Thursday evening, Friday evening, weekend and holiday, and nights — rather than a base code plus a separate premium. Special visit premiums are a different mechanism and apply when you are called in.

How is critical care time billed in the emergency department?

G395A covers the first 15 minutes of critical care and G391A each subsequent 15-minute unit. The time has to be documented and devoted to that patient. The units are the common source of rejections, because the recorded time has to support the number claimed.

What gets missed most in emergency billing?

Time-band assessment codes on shifts that cross midnight, critical care units under-claimed against documented time, and the ECG professional component. All three are omissions rather than errors, so nothing on the Remittance Advice tells you they happened.

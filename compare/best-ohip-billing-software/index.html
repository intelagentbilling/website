OHIP billing software in Ontario, compared

Five providers, one consolidation, and a market where almost everyone charges a similar percentage. The differences are in mobile, groups, ownership and what happens past the cap.

Published October 11, 20269 min read
In short

Ontario has a handful of OHIP billing providers and they price within a hair of each other at the self-service tier. The real differences are: whether there is a native mobile app, whether the monthly fee is capped, whether group and departmental billing is properly supported, and whether the company is independent or bank-owned. MDBilling.ca is no longer a separate product — RBC merged it into Dr.Bill.

This page sets out what each provider publishes about itself, with dates and sources. We have not scored anyone, invented feature matrices, or repeated figures we could not find on the vendor's own site.

The consolidation worth knowing about

If you are comparing Dr.Bill against MDBilling.ca, you are comparing one company against itself. RBC acquired Dr.Bill in October 2019 and MDBilling.ca in October 2022, and the two have since been merged — mdbilling.ca/pricing now redirects to dr-bill.ca.

That leaves Ontario with one bank-owned provider and a set of independents. Whether that is a point in anybody's favour depends on what you value; it is simply a fact about the market that was not true three years ago.

Who each provider suits

IntelAGENT

Built for physicians who bill away from a desk — emergency departments, inpatient services, hospital groups and departments. Operating since 2016 and used by more than 2,000 Ontario physicians. Self-service is 0.25% of paid claims capped at $39.99 a month; Premium is 2% and includes a dedicated billing agent. Native apps on iOS and Android with health card scanning, a web portal, and group and departmental billing with remittances split back to the rendering physician. Three months free.

Dr.Bill

The largest and best-known, now part of RBC. Publishes support for 60+ specialties, operates in more than one province, and offers both a mobile app and a desktop product. Essentials is 0.25% of paid claims with no published cap; Comprehensive is 1.95% and adds a billing team. Billing-agent help on Essentials is charged hourly. The lack of a published cap is the main thing to model before committing if you bill at volume.

SnapBill

Independent, built by Ontario family physicians, and the cheapest published self-service rate in the province at 0.2% capped at $34.99 plus HST. Explicitly states no setup fees, no minimums and no contracts, offers a 50% new-graduate discount in the first year, and gives three months of Premium free. It is a web application and does not advertise a native mobile app, and its own site describes EMR integrations with OSCAR and WELL as in active development rather than live. Ontario only.

MedConcierge

Independent, Ontario only, designed by an Ontario physician, and sold as a single plan at 0.25% capped at $40 plus HST with three months free. Publishes a long feature list including health card validation, Remittance Advice Inquiries, in-app resubmission, batch and recurring billing, macros, IFH autofill, MCEDT document upload and automatic reconciliation, with Canadian hosting and PHIPA/PIPEDA compliance stated. It does not clearly state whether there is a native mobile app.

CAB.md

From Clinical Support Systems in Kingston, Ontario. Publishes iOS and Android apps, automatic MCEDT upload and download, shift-premium prompting and patient scheduling, and claims 8,500+ physicians. The notable gap is pricing: as of October 2026 there is none on its own website. Third-party listings quote figures, but we have not been able to verify them against the vendor and so are not repeating them. Ask CAB.md directly before comparing them on cost.

Why we are not ranking these. A ranked list would require us to score things we cannot verify — support quality, uptime, how a given feature actually behaves. What we can do honestly is lay out what each company publishes, date it, and say where the gaps are.

Where the real differences are

  1. The cap

    At the self-service tier, every Ontario provider that publishes pricing charges between 0.2% and 0.25%. That is a rounding error. Whether the fee stops at roughly $35–$40 a month is not. See pricing compared.

  2. Mobile

    If you bill from a ward, a department or a car, a web app on a phone browser is a different experience from a native app that scans a health card. Three of the five publish native apps.

  3. Groups and departments

    Billing under a ministry group number, and splitting the group's remittance back to the physician who rendered each service, is a specific capability. It is not implied by "supports groups" on a feature list — ask how reversals are attributed.

  4. Geography

    Most of these providers are Ontario-only. If you might practise in another province, that narrows the list sharply.

  5. Ownership and longevity

    One provider is bank-owned; the rest are independent. One has been operating since 2016. These are facts, not verdicts, but they are the kind of fact that is awkward to discover later.

Questions to put to any provider

  • Is the fee on paid or submitted claims, and is it capped?
  • Is there a setup fee, a minimum, or a contract term?
  • Who works my rejections, and is that included or charged hourly?
  • Do you download the claims error report and the Remittance Advice automatically?
  • Can you split a group remittance back to the rendering physician, including reversals?
  • Can I export my patient list and claim history, and in what format?
  • What happens to my data if I leave?

Frequently asked questions

What is the best OHIP billing software?

There is no single answer, because the providers differ most on things that matter to some practices and not others: whether there is a native mobile app, whether group and departmental billing is supported, whether the monthly fee is capped, and whether the company operates outside Ontario. Pick on the two or three of those that describe your week.

Who owns Dr.Bill and MDBilling.ca?

Both are owned by RBC. RBC acquired Dr.Bill in October 2019 and MDBilling.ca in October 2022, and MDBilling was subsequently merged into Dr.Bill — the mdbilling.ca pricing page now redirects to dr-bill.ca.

Which OHIP billing providers are independent?

As of October 2026, IntelAGENT, SnapBill, MedConcierge and CAB.md are independent of a bank. Dr.Bill and MDBilling.ca are owned by RBC. Whether that matters is a judgement call, but it is a factual difference worth knowing.

Which OHIP billing software has a mobile app?

IntelAGENT, Dr.Bill and CAB.md publish native mobile apps. SnapBill is a web application and does not advertise a native app. MedConcierge does not clearly state whether a native app exists. Check the App Store and Google Play directly if a phone app matters to you.

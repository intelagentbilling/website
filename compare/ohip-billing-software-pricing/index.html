What OHIP billing software costs

Almost every Ontario provider charges a percentage of your billings. The only question that matters is whether that percentage stops at a cap.

Published October 11, 20267 min read
In short

Ontario OHIP billing software is almost universally priced as a percentage of paid claims. Self-service tiers cluster around 0.2% to 0.25%, and several cap the monthly charge somewhere in the $35–$40 range. Full-service tiers with a billing agent run 1.5% to 2% and are generally uncapped. The cap is the single biggest determinant of what you actually pay, and it matters more the more you bill.

Percentage pricing makes providers look similar. They are not similar, because 0.25% capped and 1.95% uncapped describe completely different bills at the same billing volume.

Why the cap is the whole story

A percentage with no ceiling is a tax on your success. A percentage with a ceiling is a subscription that happens to start small.

The arithmetic is simple enough to do in your head. At 0.25% capped at $39.99, the cap binds once your paid claims pass roughly $16,000 a month. Beyond that point, the fee is flat whatever you bill.

Costs that are not in the percentage

The headline rate is not always the bill. Things worth asking about:

  • Rejection handling on the cheap tier. Both Dr.Bill and SnapBill publish an hourly rate — $50/hour at the time of writing — for billing-agent help on their self-service tiers. If your rejection volume is high, that is not an edge case.
  • Setup or onboarding fees. SnapBill explicitly states there are none. Others do not address it either way.
  • Minimums. A monthly minimum turns a percentage into a floor. Ask.
  • Contract length. SnapBill states no contracts. Check what notice is required elsewhere.
  • Per-physician vs per-account pricing for groups. A group of twelve priced per physician is a different proposition from one priced on group billings.

What to weigh besides price

At the self-service tier the price difference between providers is a few dollars a month. That is not a decision. What actually differs:

  • Whether there is a native mobile app, and whether it can scan a health card.
  • Whether group and departmental billing is supported, including splitting remittances back to the rendering physician.
  • Whether the provider works in provinces other than Ontario, if you might move.
  • How long the provider has been operating, and who owns it.
  • What happens to your data if you leave.

Those are covered in how to choose OHIP billing software and the best OHIP billing software in Ontario.

Frequently asked questions

How much does OHIP billing software cost in Ontario?

Self-service tiers are typically priced between 0.2% and 0.25% of paid claims, and several cap the monthly charge in the $35 to $40 range. Full-service tiers that include a billing agent are typically 1.5% to 2% of paid claims, uncapped. One provider publishes no pricing on its own site at all.

What does a monthly cap on billing software actually save?

Everything above the point where the cap binds. At 0.25% capped at $39.99, the cap takes effect at roughly $16,000 of paid claims a month. A physician billing $40,000 a month pays $39.99 under the cap and $100 without it, for identical work.

Is billing software priced on submitted or paid claims?

The Ontario providers that publish their basis all price on paid claims, which means a rejected claim that is never resubmitted costs you nothing in fees. It is still worth confirming in writing, because the difference is real on a practice with a high rejection rate.

Do OHIP billing providers charge HST on top?

Several state their fees are plus HST. Compare on the same basis — a rate quoted before tax against one quoted after tax is not a like-for-like comparison. Check each provider's own pricing page for how they state it.

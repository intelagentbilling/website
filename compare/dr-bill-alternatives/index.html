Dr.Bill alternatives in Ontario

RBC now owns both Dr.Bill and MDBilling.ca. These are the providers still operating independently in Ontario, and what each one is for.

Published October 11, 20266 min read
In short

Four Ontario OHIP billing providers operate independently of RBC: IntelAGENT, SnapBill, MedConcierge and CAB.md. All but CAB.md publish capped self-service pricing within a few dollars of each other. MDBilling.ca is not an alternative to Dr.Bill — RBC bought it in 2022 and merged the two.

People search for alternatives for specific reasons, not general ones. The three that come up most are the absence of a published cap on Dr.Bill's Essentials tier, hourly charges for billing-agent help on that tier, and a preference for an independent provider. If none of those describe you, Dr.Bill may well be the right answer and this page will not change that.

What each one is for

IntelAGENT — hospital, group and departmental billing

Operating since 2016, independent, used by more than 2,000 Ontario physicians. Self-service is 0.25% of paid claims capped at $39.99 a month, with support included rather than billed hourly; Premium is 2% with a dedicated billing agent. Native iOS and Android apps with health card scanning, a web portal, Epic via SMART on FHIR, and group and departmental billing that splits remittances back to the rendering physician. Three months free. Ontario only.

SnapBill — the cheapest published rate

From ViteMD Inc., built by Ontario family physicians. 0.2% capped at $34.99 plus HST on Essentials, 1.5% plus HST on Premium. States no setup fees, no minimums and no contracts, three months of Premium free, a 50% new-graduate discount in the first year and a $150 referral credit. A web application with no native mobile app advertised; OSCAR and WELL integrations described as in active development. Best suited to clinic-based practice. Full comparison: SnapBill vs IntelAGENT.

MedConcierge — one plan, long feature list

Independent, Ontario only, designed by an Ontario physician. A single plan at 0.25% capped at $40 plus HST, with three months free. Publishes health card validation, Remittance Advice Inquiries, in-app resubmission, batch and recurring billing, macros, IFH autofill, MCEDT document upload and automatic reconciliation, with Canadian hosting and PHIPA/PIPEDA compliance stated. Does not clearly state whether there is a native mobile app.

CAB.md — apps, but no published price

From Clinical Support Systems in Kingston, Ontario. Publishes iOS and Android apps, automatic MCEDT upload and download, shift-premium prompting and patient scheduling, and claims 8,500+ physicians. As of October 2026 there is no pricing on its own site. Third-party listings quote figures we have not been able to verify with the vendor, so we are not reproducing them. Ask CAB.md directly.

Why no scores. We are not going to rate competitors on support quality, uptime or ease of use, because we cannot verify any of it. What we can do is state what each company publishes, date it, and say where the gaps are.

The three reasons people actually move

  1. The cap

    Dr.Bill's Essentials tier publishes no monthly cap. At 0.25% uncapped, $60,000 of paid claims a month costs $150 in fees. Under a $39.99 cap it costs $39.99. For a part-time practice this is immaterial; for a full-time hospital physician it is a standing difference. See pricing compared.

  2. Hourly charges for rejection help

    Both Dr.Bill and SnapBill publish an hourly rate — $50/hour at the time of writing — for billing-agent assistance on their self-service tiers. If your practice generates rejections routinely, confirm what counts as billable before you assume the headline rate is your whole cost.

  3. Independence

    Some physicians prefer not to have their billing data inside a bank. That is a preference, not an argument, and it is worth being honest that it is one. RBC owns Dr.Bill and MDBilling.ca; the four providers above are independent.

If you do move

Switching is a designee change, not a re-registration. Your OHIP billing number, GO Secure account, EDT enrolment and the bank account the ministry deposits into stay exactly where they are.

The one thing to get right is the overlap. Add the new designee first, stop entering new claims in the old system, let the old system's outstanding claims adjudicate and be reconciled, export your history, then remove the old designation. Cutting over on the 17th of a month with claims half-submitted is how claims get lost. Full sequence: switching OHIP billing software.

Frequently asked questions

What are the alternatives to Dr.Bill in Ontario?

IntelAGENT, SnapBill, MedConcierge and CAB.md are the Ontario OHIP billing providers operating independently of RBC as of October 2026. MDBilling.ca is not an alternative — RBC acquired it in 2022 and merged it into Dr.Bill.

Why would I switch away from Dr.Bill?

The three reasons physicians usually give are the absence of a published monthly cap on the Essentials tier, hourly charges for billing-agent help on that tier, and a preference for an independent provider. Whether any of those apply depends on your billing volume and your rejection rate.

Is switching from Dr.Bill difficult?

No. Your billing number, GO Secure account and EDT enrolment stay with the ministry. Switching means adding a new MCEDT designee and removing the old one once outstanding claims have settled. The risk is a gap during the handover, which is avoided by overlapping rather than cutting over.

Which Dr.Bill alternative is cheapest?

On published self-service rates as of October 2026, SnapBill at 0.2% capped at $34.99 plus HST. IntelAGENT is 0.25% capped at $39.99 and MedConcierge is 0.25% capped at $40 plus HST. CAB.md publishes no pricing. The spread between the capped options is a few dollars a month.

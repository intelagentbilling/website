OHIP billing by specialty

The Schedule of Benefits is the same for everyone. What differs by specialty is which part of it you live in, and which part you keep forgetting.

Published October 11, 2026

Generic OHIP billing advice is close to useless, because the problems are specialty-shaped. An emergency physician's whole difficulty is the time band on an assessment code. An anaesthesiologist's is the extra units. A psychiatrist's is unit limits. None of those overlap.

Each page below covers the codes at the centre of that specialty, with fees from the Schedule of Benefits effective July 1, 2026, the premium mechanism that applies, and the specific things that get under-billed. Fees are the ministry's and identical across every billing provider — the differences are in eligibility rules, which we point at the Schedule for rather than paraphrase.

Billing as a group or department

If several physicians in your specialty bill under a shared group number, or an administrator bills for a whole department, the specialty rules still apply but the plumbing changes — remittances arrive against the group and have to be split back to the rendering physician.

OHIP billing guides

Reference material for Ontario physicians and the people who bill for them. Written from what actually turns up in remittance files, not from a brochure.

Published October 11, 2026

OHIP billing is not complicated so much as undocumented. The rules exist, in the Schedule of Benefits and in ministry publications, but almost nothing explains the sequence — what to do first, what comes back, and what to do about it when it does.

These guides fill that gap. Each one is self-contained, cites the ministry source it relies on, and says plainly where a rule is specific enough that you should check the current Schedule yourself.

Billing by specialty

Fee codes, premiums and the services that go unbilled differ sharply between specialties. The specialty pages cover emergency medicine, internal medicine, psychiatry, anaesthesia, general surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics and family practice.

A note on accuracy

Fee amounts, time bands and ministry processes change. Where a guide names a specific amount or rule, it says when it was accurate and links to the ministry source. Where ordering reflects our own observation of real remittance data rather than a published ranking, it says that too.

Nothing here is a substitute for the OHIP Schedule of Benefits. If a guide and the Schedule disagree, the Schedule is right.

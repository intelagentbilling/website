OHIP billing deadlines and stale dating

A claim has a shelf life. Miss the window and the service is unbillable — not reduced, not appealable in the ordinary way, just gone.

Published October 11, 20266 min read
In short

Two different clocks. The submission window runs from the service date and decides whether a claim can be paid at all — miss it and the claim is stale dated and refused. The monthly cut-off decides which payment run a claim lands in, and missing it only delays you. Most lost revenue comes from the first clock, and almost all of it is avoidable.

The submission window is three months from the date a service is rendered, for services rendered on or after April 1, 2023. It was six months before that, and the longer figure is still quoted in a lot of places. The cut-off dates are set by the ministry and change, so take those from the source. What is worth understanding is the structure, because the structure is what determines which claims in your practice are actually at risk.

Check the current rules. The submission window is set out in Regulation 552 under the Health Insurance Act, and the ministry publishes the payment schedule on its physician payment page. Those are authoritative; this page is not.

The clock starts at the service date

This is the single most important thing and the one most often misunderstood. The window is counted from the date the service was rendered, not from:

  • the date you entered the claim into your billing system,
  • the date the claim was first submitted,
  • the date the patient was discharged, or
  • the date a rejection came back.

That last one is the trap. A claim submitted on time, rejected, and left sitting in a rejection queue for weeks is still ageing against its original service date. You do not get a fresh window because the first attempt failed. Correcting a rejection two months after it arrived can put the correction outside the deadline even though the original submission was well inside it.

Which claims actually age out

In practice, stale dating hits a predictable set of claims:

  1. Rejections nobody worked

    The largest category by far. The claim was submitted, came back with an explanatory code, and was never corrected. Every week it sits there is a week off the window. See why claims get rejected.

  2. Encounters never entered

    A shift billed from memory, a locum week reconstructed late, a stack of paper that did not get typed up. These never enter the system at all, so no deadline tracking catches them. Reducing missed billings covers finding them.

  3. Claims waiting on something

    A missing health card number, an unconfirmed referring physician, an admission date nobody could find. Claims held pending information age at exactly the same rate as claims being actively worked.

  4. Handover gaps

    Switching billing providers, changing administrators, or a biller leaving. Claims in flight during a transition are the ones that fall between two systems. Switching providers sets out how to avoid the gap.

  5. Long inpatient stays

    Where billing is done at discharge rather than as care is delivered, the earliest service dates on a months-long admission can be close to the deadline by the time anything is submitted.

The monthly cut-off is a different thing

The ministry adjudicates claims in payment runs. Claims accepted before the cut-off for a run are adjudicated in that run and appear on that month's Remittance Advice; claims arriving after it go into the next one.

Missing a cut-off costs you timing, not money. It matters if you are managing cash flow or reconciling against an expected deposit, and it matters a great deal if you assume a claim failed because it did not appear on the Remittance Advice you were expecting. A claim submitted a day after the cut-off is not missing; it is in the next run.

This is a common source of false alarms. Before chasing a claim that is not on the RA, check which run it should have been in. See reading a Remittance Advice.

A useful habit. Submit continuously rather than in a monthly push. Claims submitted as the work happens are never near either clock, and a submission problem shows up within days instead of at month end when there is no time to fix it.

Working rejections against the clock

Because a rejected claim keeps ageing, rejection handling should be ordered by remaining window rather than by when the rejection arrived or how much the claim is worth.

A practical priority order:

  • Oldest service date first, regardless of value. A $46.65 claim about to age out is worth more than a $342.25 claim with months left, because the second one will still be there tomorrow.
  • Then batches. Rejections often come in families — one wrong facility or group number produces dozens of identical rejections that are all fixed the same way. Doing the family together is faster than doing them in arrival order.
  • Then everything else.

If you cannot see remaining window per claim, you cannot do this, which is the practical argument for tracking it rather than eyeballing dates.

What to do about claims that already aged out

Pushing it back through the normal channel just gets it refused again. There are two real routes, and which one applies turns on a single question: did the claim ever reach OHIP inside the window?

It was submitted on time and rejected

This is the recoverable case. A claim submitted within the three months that came back on an error report can be corrected and resubmitted after the window has closed. The ministry asks for supporting documentation proving the original submission was on time — the error report and the claim identifying data — with the corrected claims sent as a stale dated claim file through MCEDT, followed by an email to ClaimsManagement@ontario.ca. Do it as soon as possible after the rejection date.

It was never submitted in time

This one is a letter rather than a resubmission, and the bar is high. The ministry will consider paying a stale dated claim only where extenuating circumstances prevented submission, which it defines as extraordinary and unusual events arising suddenly and unexpectedly and beyond the provider's control. Its guidance says plainly that administrative or operational errors inside a physician's office do not qualify, which rules out most backlogs.

The request goes on letterhead to ClaimsManagement@ontario.ca and has to carry:

  • your full name, address, phone number and six-digit billing number, plus the four-digit group number where one applies;
  • a list of the stale dated claims, each with the patient's health number, the fee schedule codes and the dates of service;
  • the extenuating reasons that prevented submission inside the window, with supporting documentation where it exists;
  • your signature, or a group representative's — the ministry does not accept a billing agent or secretary signature.

The useful work after discovering an aged-out batch is diagnostic rather than recoverable: find out what mechanism let those claims sit, and close it. Almost always the answer is that nobody was looking at the rejection queue, or that a category of encounter was never being entered at all.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to submit an OHIP claim?

Three months from the date of service, for services rendered on or after April 1, 2023. Services rendered before that date had six months. The window is counted from the date of service, not the date you entered the claim, and a claim that arrives later is stale dated.

What does stale dated mean on an OHIP claim?

It means the claim reached the ministry after the submission deadline for its service date. The claim is refused outright rather than reduced, and resubmitting it unchanged does not help — the service date is what aged out.

What is the OHIP monthly cut-off?

Claims accepted by the ministry's cut-off are adjudicated in that month's payment run and appear on that month's Remittance Advice. Claims arriving after it roll to the next run. The cut-off decides when you are paid, not whether.

Can a stale dated claim ever be paid?

Sometimes. A claim submitted inside the window and rejected to an error report can be corrected and resubmitted afterwards, with the error report as proof it was on time. A claim never submitted in time needs a letter to ClaimsManagement@ontario.ca showing extenuating circumstances, which the ministry defines narrowly and does not extend to office errors.
